# The app, read as a person using it — 2026-09-12

The owner, 12 September: "the application should be given some love, it works well
but it has its rough edges and isn't the best UX."

Two audits preceded this one and neither asked this question.
The first read the tree for structure after the port to Rust; the second
read `web/` for duplication, dead comments and missing tests, and all
twenty-four of its findings landed. Both were about the SOURCE. Not one of them
was about what the app is like to open on a phone, which is the thing this file
is about and the reason its findings do not overlap theirs.

The evidence is `web/e2e/shots.test.ts` — the gallery, twenty-six pictures, every
destination at both widths in both themes, taken against the fixture chain at
this commit. Where a finding below quotes a measurement it was read off those
pictures rather than estimated from the CSS.

## What is already right, so it is not re-litigated

The graph is the product working: five todos' fulfillment over months, chain
events as dots on the lines, a zoom and a reset. The Inbox draws a held batch
with the secretary's reason above it and the diff underneath, which is exactly
the thing §10.6 was built to produce. The staleness banner dates itself. Dark
mode is not an afterthought — the cards, the chips and the percentage colours
all hold their contrast. The percentage leads every card and is the biggest
thing on it, which is correct, because it is the sort key.

None of that needs defending. What follows is where the app spends the reader's
attention on the wrong thing.

## Findings

1. **The todo page leads with the id and the arithmetic, not the todo.**
   Open `file-permit` on a phone and the fold reads: the slug as the heading, the
   price, three chips, an "Ask the secretary" button, a provenance sentence,
   and then a thirty-day fulfillment chart. **The body text — "File the permit
   renewal" — is not on the screen at all.** You tap a todo and the one thing
   you cannot see is what it says. Below the chart, "Why this number" opens on
   raw term syntax (`decay = 12.9% end=0.05 endDate="2026-09-14T20:07:23"`),
   which is the evaluator's spelling and not a sentence.
   *Direction:* the body is the heading and the slug is a subtitle; the chart
   and the explanation move below the fold, where somebody asking "why that
   number" will scroll for them. The FPL term stays — it is evidence and this
   app shows its working — but under a disclosure that opens on prose.
   **This is the sharpest one: it is the screen reached most often and the only
   one that hides its own subject.**

2. **The list spends a third of the phone on chrome before the first todo.**
   Measured on `phone-light-list.png` at 390×844: the app bar, the "Todos"
   heading, the search field, a row of four state chips, a row of four category
   chips, and a count with an explanatory line come to roughly 280px of 844
   before any content. Three cards fit. The life chain currently holds
   forty-five open todos, so the ordinary act of looking at what is urgent is
   three at a time.
   Each card then spends a whole footer row on its slug and a chevron, when the
   whole card is already the link (`layout.ts` made it one deliberately).
   *Direction:* filters collapse to a single control naming the ACTIVE filter
   and open on demand; the slug moves into the card's header row beside the
   chips; the ordering explainer becomes first-run rather than permanent. Those
   three together roughly double what a thumb can see without scrolling.

3. **The app explains its architecture where the reader came to act.**
   The Secretary page — empty, with no conversation started — closes with five
   lines on prodrome compiled to WebAssembly, verifying objects, recomputing
   knots, and "one evaluator, on two machines". The Graph page carries a
   permanent paragraph on what low means. The list restates "lowest fulfillment
   first — low means urgent" on every visit. The todo page explains that the
   browser interpolates between knots and computes no fulfillment of its own.
   Every one of those sentences is true, well written, and load-bearing to the
   project's claims about itself. None of them is what somebody opening the page
   came for, and on a phone each costs part of the fold permanently.
   *Direction:* they are documentation, so give them somewhere to be — one
   About destination — and let each page keep at most one line, dismissible.
   ⚠️ NOT a deletion: the claim that the browser computes nothing of its own is
   the system's whole argument, and an app that stopped saying it would be
   quieter and less honest. It needs a home, not a grave.

4. **The graph draws five anonymous lines.**
   Colour is the only thing distinguishing them and the legend is a collapsed
   drawer reading "Todos · 5 selected". So the page that is the best argument
   for the whole design cannot be read without a second tap.
   *Direction:* label the lines at their right-hand end, where each one already
   terminates at a distinct height; fall back to the drawer when they collide.

5. **Two controls switch chain and neither says how they relate.**
   `Project` is a bottom-bar destination that calls `setChain`, and the More
   sheet carries a `life`/`suzatary` segmented control. Both are deliberate —
   the 09-10 pass made Project a destination "rather than a switcher buried in
   the sheet", and kept the switcher for a third chain. What was not decided is
   what the switcher should SAY while the Project tab is the active
   destination, and today it says `life`, which is not what the page is showing.
   *Direction:* the switcher reflects the chain in view, or it is hidden while a
   chain-specific destination is active. A control that disagrees with the page
   is worse than no control.

6. **The theme control holds permanent space in the rail and the sheet.**
   System/Light/Dark is pinned at the bottom of the desktop rail and again at
   the bottom of the phone's More sheet — prime persistent real estate for a
   setting touched about twice in a lifetime.
   *Direction:* into the About destination from finding 3, which it can share.

## Not findings

The bottom bar's four slots plus More: settled on 09-10 against the rule "what a
phone opens the app FOR", and nothing here disturbs it. The card's colour bar
and the big percentage: correct, and the reason the list scans at all. The
staleness banner appearing on every page: it is a claim about the whole fold, so
every page is where it belongs.

## Order

1 first, alone — it is the most-used screen and it is a rearrangement rather
than new machinery. Then 2, which is three separable commits (the filter
control, the card's slug row, the first-run explainer) and is the one a person
feels daily. Then 3 and 6 together, since 6 lands in the destination 3 creates.
Then 4. Then 5, last, because it is the only one whose right answer is not yet
obvious and it should not block the rest.

Every step keeps the gate: `scripts/web-test.sh`, `scripts/web-e2e.sh`, and the
gallery re-taken and looked at, because a design pass is judged by a person
looking at it.
